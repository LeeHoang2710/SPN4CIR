\begin{abstract}
    The Composed Image Retrieval (CIR) task involves retrieving target images based on a composed query of a reference image and modified text. This task is challenging due to the scarcity of positive examples and the limited scope of negative sampling techniques. This study aims to improve the effectiveness of contrastive learning for CIR by introducing a multi-modal large language model to generate high-quality triplets and a two-stage fine-tuning framework. We leverage pretrained vision-language models, such as BLIP-2, to generate and concatenate sentence-level prompts with the relative caption. Our method scales the number of negatives using static representations. Our approach achieves state-of-the-art performance on the FashionIQ and CIRR datasets, boosting the R@10 and R@50 metric across various baseline models. Additionally, our method enables robust zero-shot CIR capabilities. These advancements demonstrate the potential of scalable triplet construction and enhanced fine-tuning to drive significant progress in CIR tasks, even in low-resource scenarios. Injecting knowledge about angle transformations into the CIR model to distinguish between different view angles and perspectives holds significant potential.
\end{abstract}
