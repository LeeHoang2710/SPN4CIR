\subsection{Composed Image Retrieval (CIR)}
Composed Image Retrieval (CIR) combines a query image with modifying text to retrieve target images, necessitating effective fusion of visual and textual modalities. Early approaches~\cite{vo2019} explored residual-based methods to integrate visual cues with textual modifiers. More recent works leverage large-scale vision-language models: Liu et al.~\cite{liu2021} introduced CIRPLANT using transformer-based adaptation, while Baldrati et al.~\cite{baldrati2022,baldrati2023} exploited CLIP for robust feature fusion. Xu et al.~\cite{xu2023} proposed ComqueryFormer, a unified transformer architecture with global-local alignment, and Zhao et al.~\cite{zhao2022} incorporated progressive learning with adaptive weighting for hybrid queries. Additional innovations include sentence-level prompting~\cite{bai2023}, zero-shot methods~\cite{saito2023}, and context-aware mapping techniques~\cite{tang2024}, as well as extensions to video retrieval~\cite{ventura2024}.

\subsection{Fashion Image Retrieval (FIR)}
The challenge of bridging the semantic gap between low-level visual features and high-level fashion concepts is addressed in Fashion Image Retrieval (FIR). Research highlights low-level features and optimisation algorithms for semantic recognition~\cite{hare2006,karmokar2013}, while later advancements include semantic fusion networks~\cite{liu2020} and compositional approaches~\cite{valle2018} to capture outfit constituents. Interactive retrieval systems supported by datasets such as Fashion IQ~\cite{wu2020} have emerged, utilizing methods like mix attention-based CNNs for brand logo recognition~\cite{liu2021} and memory-based models for multi-turn feedback~\cite{pal2023}. These studies underscore the importance of semantic understanding and iterative user feedback in refining retrieval results and enhancing overall retrieval efficiency.

\subsection{Data Generation and Negative Sampling for CIR}
Data Generation and Negative Sampling for CIR have recently gained prominence. Synthetic data methods, such as CompoDiff~\cite{gu2024}, use latent diffusion to automate triplet generation, while approaches like MagicLens~\cite{zhang2024} and InstructPix2Pix~\cite{brooks2023} exploit self-supervised learning and generative models for synthesizing embeddings and compositional queries. Effective negative sampling remains crucial for contrastive learning; techniques including targeted sampling~\cite{feng2024,zhou2024}, SimCLR~\cite{chen2020}, non-parametric instance discrimination~\cite{wu2018}, conditional negative sampling~\cite{wu2020conditional}, and contrastive hashing with vision transformers~\cite{ren2022} have all contributed to learning robust and transferable representations.
